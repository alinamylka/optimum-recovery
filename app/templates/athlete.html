How to read this {% if model.key == 'debt' %}

Readiness (−9…+9): today's HRV, resting HR and stress against the athlete's own 60-day baseline. Positive = better than usual.

Fatigue debt: builds on negative days, drains on positive ones, and drains slower once it is deep. Above {{ params.amber|int }} load is no longer absorbed day to day (overreaching); above {{ params.red|int }} there is a risk of non-functional overreaching. A green line marks a full recovery: the debt went back to zero.

Rebound: a sudden HRV jump while still in debt — often the body's reaction to overload, not freshness.

Chronic creep: the weekly HRV average sits below the normal range even though no single day looks bad.

{% else %}

Top strip: the day's decision. Green = high intensity as planned, amber = low intensity or rest.

HRV: dots are daily readings, the line is the 7-day average of ln(RMSSD) shown in ms, the grey band is the normal range (smallest worthwhile change, mean ± 0.5 SD). The band comes from the first 2 weeks, then is recalculated every 4 weeks from the 4 weeks before — so it moves in steps.

Leaving the band upwards also means an easy day: in the papers a high HRV is not read as "extra fresh".

CV: day-to-day variation of HRV over 7 days (Plews 2012). A falling average with a rising CV is a warning sign; the papers give no threshold, so it does not change the decision.

Resting HR is shown for context only; this method decides on HRV alone.
